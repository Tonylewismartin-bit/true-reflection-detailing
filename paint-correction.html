What Is Paint Correction?

Professional Paint Correction & Machine Polishing

Paint correction is a careful machine-polishing process used to improve the appearance of vehicle paintwork by reducing visible defects such as swirl marks, light scratches, oxidation and wash-induced marring.

Abrasive polishing removes a small amount of the paint surface to refine defects. On clear-coated paint, that means working within the clear coat, so its condition limits how much correction is appropriate. Some marks are better left in place than pursued with increasingly aggressive polishing.

01

Reduce Swirl Marks

Careful machine polishing can significantly reduce the fine circular marks commonly caused by poor washing and drying techniques.

02

Improve Gloss & Clarity

Refining the paint surface can restore depth, clarity and reflection that has been lost through everyday wear and contamination.

03

Vehicle-Specific Approach

Every vehicle is assessed individually so the polishing process is appropriate for the condition and limitations of its paintwork.

Common Paintwork Problems

Which Defects Can Paint Correction Improve?

The type and depth of a mark matter more than its size. These are common reasons to arrange an assessment, rather than guarantees of removal.

Swirl Marks & Wash Marring

Fine swirls and wash marks can make reflections look hazy, especially in direct light. Appropriate machine polishing can reduce these shallow defects. The result depends on their depth and the condition of the finish; careful washing afterwards helps limit new marks.

Light Scratches & Deeper Damage

Shallow scratches may be improved or removed where there is enough sound paint to work with. Scratches through the clear coat, chips and exposed primer or metal cannot be repaired by polishing. Some damage needs touch-up work or refinishing by a paint repair specialist.

Oxidation & Faded Paintwork

Dullness and surface oxidation can sometimes respond to polishing, but faded paint has more than one possible cause. Peeling, failing clear coat or deeper deterioration will not be restored by machine polishing. We assess the finish before recommending correction.

Bird-Dropping Etching

Removing a bird dropping and correcting the mark it leaves are different tasks. Once residue is cleaned away, a dull outline or etched patch may remain. Light etching may improve with polishing, while deeper damage may need to remain or require paint repair.

Tree Sap & Bonded Contamination

Sap and other bonded deposits need suitable cleaning and decontamination before polishing is considered. Correction is for defects left in the paint, rather than a substitute for removing deposits. Any staining or etching that remains is assessed separately.

Haze & Polishing Marks

An uneven or hazy finish can come from fine surface defects or previous polishing. Refinement may improve clarity where the paint condition allows it. Tell us about previous correction or resprayed panels so we can take that history into account.

Our Process

Our Paint Correction Process

We assess the paintwork before deciding how much correction is appropriate. The aim is to achieve a noticeable improvement without unnecessarily removing clear coat.

1. Paintwork Assessment

We inspect swirl marks, scratches, oxidation and other visible defects, taking the paint condition and any known repair history into account before recommending the level of correction.

2. Safe Wash & Decontamination

The paintwork is thoroughly cleaned and decontaminated so bonded contamination does not interfere with the polishing process.

3. Machine Polishing

Suitable polishing combinations are selected according to the condition of the paint, with the aim of safely reducing defects and improving clarity.

4. Refinement & Protection

Once polishing is complete, the finish is assessed and suitable protection can be applied. For longer-term protection, you can also consider our ceramic coating service.
Realistic Results

A Better Finish, With Sensible Limits.

There is no fixed percentage of defect removal that suits every vehicle. Paint type, previous polishing, repairs and the depth of damage all affect the result. We explain what can reasonably be improved and which marks may remain before agreeing the work.

Paint correction does not replace missing paint or repair peeling clear coat. Protection applied afterwards does not make the finish scratch-proof, and poor washing can introduce new swirls.

Frequently Asked Questions

Paint Correction & Machine Polishing FAQs

Does paint correction remove swirl marks?

It can reduce or remove many shallow swirl marks through careful machine polishing. The outcome depends on the depth of the marks and the condition of the paint. We do not guarantee complete removal of every defect.

Can machine polishing remove scratches?

Polishing can improve or remove some light scratches within the clear coat. Deeper scratches may remain, and damage through the paint needs a different repair. Our car scratch guide helps you describe the mark before an assessment.

Can you improve oxidation or faded paint?

Some surface oxidation and dullness can be improved, depending on the paint type and condition. Fading caused by deeper deterioration or failing clear coat cannot be fixed by polishing. Peeling paint needs assessment by a paint repair specialist.

Can polishing remove bird-dropping marks?

Once the residue has been cleaned away, shallow etching may respond to polishing. Deeper etching or permanent damage may remain. We assess the affected area rather than promise that every mark can be removed.

Does tree sap need paint correction?

Not necessarily. Sap is a deposit that needs appropriate removal first. If the paint underneath is sound, correction may not be needed. Any remaining staining or etching is assessed after cleaning and decontamination.

What is the difference between machine polishing and paint correction?

Machine polishing is a method used to refine the paint surface. Paint correction describes work aimed at reducing visible defects such as swirls, light scratches and haze, using an approach matched to the vehicle. The amount of work depends on the finish and the result that is realistically achievable.

How much does paint correction cost and how long does it take?

The price and time depend on vehicle size, paint condition and the level of work agreed. Send vehicle details, your location and clear photographs of the affected panels. Photographs help with an initial discussion, but an inspection may be needed to confirm the scope and quotation.

Is paint correction suitable for every finish?

No. Damaged, peeling or previously heavily polished paint may limit or rule out correction. Matte and satin finishes should not be treated like gloss paint, because polishing can change their appearance. Tell us about the finish, wraps, coatings and any known repairs when enquiring.

Should I have ceramic coating after paint correction?

Ceramic coating can be considered after suitable preparation to help protect the finish and make maintenance easier. It does not remove scratches or make paint scratch-proof. See our ceramic coating service to discuss protection alongside the correction work.

Will swirl marks come back?

Marks removed by abrasive polishing do not simply reappear, but washing, drying and everyday contact can create new defects. Gentle maintenance and suitable wash equipment help preserve the result. Protection does not prevent all future scratches or wash marks.

Do you offer mobile paint correction in Bourne and Peterborough?

Yes. We provide mobile paint correction and machine polishing across Bourne, Peterborough, Stamford, Grantham, Market Deeping and surrounding areas, subject to suitable working conditions. Tell us about the space available at your home or workplace so we can confirm arrangements.

What information should I send before booking?

Send your vehicle make and model, location, photographs of the whole affected panel and close-ups of the marks. Describe what you want improved and mention previous polishing, resprays or known paint damage. This helps us assess whether correction is appropriate and discuss realistic results.
